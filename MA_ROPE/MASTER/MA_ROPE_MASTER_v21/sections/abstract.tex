\begin{abstract}
Autonomous vehicles must run diverse tasks with stringent QoS requirements that often exceed onboard computational capacity, motivating offloading to the network edge. Dense vehicular offloading can overload single-RAT V2X systems as vehicle density increases, and evaluating a policy at one operating point can conceal how quickly performance degrades under contention. We propose MA-ROPE---Multi-RAT Privacy-Preserving Federated Deep Reinforcement Learning for V2X Task Offloading---which jointly selects among LTE, ITS-G5, WiFi-6, and 5G-NR and adapts partial offload ratios via a Dueling Double DQN policy coordinated through FedAvg, without centrally pooling raw observations. Under URLLC stress at N=32 vehicles, MA-ROPE achieves 83.4\% fleet on-time delivery versus 52.5\% for single-RAT AllOffload, improves $U_{OTR}$ by 0.178, cuts energy by 25.2\% (0.706 J vs. 0.944 J), and lowers queue-classified loss to 0.85\% versus 2.14\%. We also introduce the Deadline Retention Ratio (DRR) to capture performance across fleet sizes: across a fourfold increase from N=8 to N=32, MA-ROPE retains 94.0\% of its reference-density delivery versus 59.9\% for AllOffload. The multi-RAT controller adds no measurable step-time overhead (166.6 \textmu s vs. 167.6 \textmu s), and DQN inference remains 59.83 \textmu s per agent. Centralized MADDPG attains a slightly higher $U_{OTR}$ (by 0.009) but requires centralized observations and 34\% more energy. These results show that jointly optimizing RAT selection, partial offloading, and shared MEC queue contention is necessary for deadline-compliant V2X delivery under dense conditions.
\end{abstract}
